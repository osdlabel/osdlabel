# Evidence: proofs of concept

Measurements behind the verdicts on the Roadmap tab.

Each report gives the question, what was built, the numbers, the verdict per hypothesis, and the branch. Reports land after an independent agent has rerun the benchmark and tried to break the conclusions. Timings come from a shared 4-core machine. A rerun on an otherwise idle machine reproduced the SAM numbers, then hit a time limit during video, so the other proofs of concept keep their shared-machine timings.

## Pixel pipeline: 16-bit windowing and the WebGL context budget

OpenSeadragon can render 16-bit CT and mammography with real window/level through a drawer subclass, unforked (on version 5 the subclass also reads two undocumented internals). The real constraint is WebGL contexts, not pixels. Branch [claude/poc-pixel-pipeline](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/tree/claude/poc-pixel-pipeline) · [REPORT.md](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/blob/claude/poc-pixel-pipeline/apps/poc-pixel-pipeline/REPORT.md).

**Built:** `@osdlabel/pixel` (zero-dependency window/level math) and `@osdlabel/osd-pixel` (a 16-bit tile source and a WebGL2 window/level drawer), plus a harness. Existing code: +63 lines in `fabric-osd`.

| Hypothesis                                                   | Verdict   | Evidence (reviewer reproduced the counts exactly)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| PX1: CSS filters cannot window 12-16-bit data                | Confirmed | Brain window shows 6 grey levels instead of 80; within osdlabel's tone bounds, error up to 129 levels. The new paths match the DICOM model on 4,001 of 4,001 values                                                                                                                                                                          |
| PX2: CPU re-windowing is fine for CT, breaks for mammography | Partly    | Whole image per step: 4.0 ms (512×512 CT), 52.9 ms (4096×3328 mammogram). Re-windowing only drawn tiles inside OpenSeadragon: 8.9 ms at 1:1; it falls below 30 fps near 4.5 megapixels drawn per frame                                                                                                                                       |
| PX3: GPU windowing plugs in without a fork                   | Partly    | A drawer subclass and tile-source hooks, plus two undocumented internals on version 5; no texture uploads during a drag; 0.07-0.39 ms of JavaScript per step with a context per cell; overlay registered 296 of 296; runs on OpenSeadragon 6.1.1 after a port of about 190 lines (per-cell context only). 60 fps unproven: software GPU only |
| PX4: a 4×4 grid sits at the 16-context limit                 | Confirmed | 17 to 24 cells leave 1 to 8 cells blank; in the dev app one extra WebGL context blanks a cell permanently. Canvas drawer: 0 contexts. One shared context: 1 context for 24 cells                                                                                                                                                             |
| Found on the way                                             | Bug       | Swapping a cell's drawer at runtime blanks it under osdlabel's overlay (15 of 15); a 25-line `replaceDrawer` fixes it                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

**Independent review:** two blocking findings, both about the recommendation, not the measurements. The shared 16-bit drawer drops tone on 8-bit tiles, and the software GPU cannot rank the drawer options. The roadmap's recommendation was corrected accordingly: canvas drawer for ordinary cells, 16-bit drawer opt-in, a real-GPU run as the gate. The fix pass was skipped to save usage budget. The reviewer also suggested WebGPU (one device, many canvases, no context limit) and packing 16-bit tiles into ordinary 8-bit PNGs as alternatives.

## Stacks: a frame axis for pixels, one image ID per slice for annotations

One OpenSeadragon cell can scroll a 300-slice stack in place, keeping the user's zoom and rotation, with annotations drawn in the same paint as their slice. Once the review forced a fair comparison, keying annotations by one image ID per slice tied or beat a frame field on every measured cost. Branch [claude/poc-frame-axis](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/tree/claude/poc-frame-axis) · [REPORT.md](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/blob/claude/poc-frame-axis/apps/poc-frame-axis/REPORT.md).

**Built:** `@osdlabel/frame` (the frame axis) and `@osdlabel/frame-osd` (a frame source contract and a presenter that repaints one tile in place), a slice-ID series grouping, a public `frameCellOptions` provider prop (canvas drawer, Fabric caching off), and wheel and keyboard navigation in the Solid binding. Existing code: 21 files, +795/−71, every new core field optional. React was not built.

| Hypothesis                                                            | Verdict   | Evidence (Solid binding, medians of 5 runs)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| FA1: stacks fit as a frame axis plus an annotation field              | Partly    | The frame axis for pixels and navigation holds: one grid assignment, the view kept, an extension rather than a rewrite. The annotation field lost to one image ID per slice: edits 69 vs 83 ms at 50 per slice and 207 vs 407 ms at 200; 30 to 32 vs 0.3 slices/s with a per-slice limit; files 5% smaller; older readers unaffected       |
| FA2: one cell scrolls a 300-slice stack at 30 fps or better           | Partly    | 55 fps at 10 annotations per slice, 34 fps at 50, 12 to 14 fps at 200, with 0 out-of-step paints in 225 passes. It needs the canvas drawer and Fabric caching off: caching on gave 11 slices/s at 50 per slice, and the WebGL drawer on the software GPU 8 at 10 per slice                                                                 |
| FA3: an ultrasound cine loop plays at 30 fps with 5% or fewer dropped | Confirmed | 0 to 1.4% dropped at 30 fps on every canvas-drawer path, WebCodecs included, stepping on the display's frame timestamps. At 60 fps: 0.7 to 4.3% from memory, 5.7 to 11% with WebCodecs, 45% with a video element. Measured in a standalone harness, not the binding                                                                        |
| An object cache with look-ahead beats rebuilding                      | Refuted   | Rebuilding each slice's objects with caching off won every pass, for example 29.2 vs 20.2 slices/s at 50 per slice, and used less memory at 10 and 50 per slice                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Found on the way                                                      | Bugs      | Mount revived all 60,000 objects at 200 per slice (9.3 s and a stack overflow; now 3.3 s). The frame index read through Solid store proxies, about 400 ms per edit; it now reads plain data. Constraint recounts through the store cost 200 to 400 ms per edit at 60,000 annotations in either model, an existing issue that stacks expose |

**Independent review and fix:** four blocking findings. The per-slice alternative had been measured against today's viewer-reopening cell, a strawman. The recommended settings were forced through globals instead of the binding. The 50-per-slice headline did not reproduce. The object cache was not justified. The fix pass rebuilt the alternative on the same presenter, made the settings public options, re-measured with 5 runs each and dropped the cache. That reversed the annotation keying. Still unknown: React, OpenSeadragon 6's tile invalidation, and real GPUs.

## Video: frame-accurate access, OpenSeadragon as the host, keyframes as annotations

osdlabel can annotate video frame by frame with its existing OpenSeadragon, Fabric and DOM layers. WebCodecs decoding is exact, OpenSeadragon drawing each decoded frame directly stays frame-locked, and keyframes work as ordinary annotations. Branch [claude/poc-video](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/tree/claude/poc-video) · [REPORT.md](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/blob/claude/poc-video/apps/poc-video/REPORT.md).

**Built:** `@osdlabel/temporal` (frame references, the tolerant time lookup, keyframes as annotations, interpolation and visibility; 1,014 source lines), `@osdlabel/video` (decode-only over a `Demuxer` interface, with a mediabunny adapter on a subpath; 521 source lines), and a harness with 12 synthetic barcode videos plus a 1080p 60 fps source. Existing packages: 0 lines.

| Hypothesis                                                              | Verdict   | Evidence (headless Chromium, software rendering)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| VI1: a video element is not frame-accurate; WebCodecs is                | Partly    | WebCodecs was exact in 1,920 of 1,920. The element was exact in 62.6% of seeks to i / fps, 97.1% to each frame's nominal middle (misses only on variable rate), and 1,440 of 1,440 to demuxed midpoints. A forward step took 0.5 to 5.2 ms with WebCodecs against 42 to 1,092 ms with the element |
| VI2: OpenSeadragon hosts 1080p video at 30 fps with the overlay in sync | Confirmed | Drawing each decoded frame directly ran at 34 to 35 fps for a 60 fps source with 50 tracks and 100 decorations, frame-locked in 100% of screenshots including fast pans, within 1 px. A separate canvas layer ran at 35.5 to 38. No path reached 60 fps on the CPU                                |
| VI3: tracks fit in a zero-dependency temporal package                   | Confirmed | 1,000 tracks interpolate in 0.18 ms per frame for rectangles, 0.49 for mixed shapes and 1.85 for polygons with unequal vertex counts. Aligned polygons keep their area; vertex-by-vertex interpolation collapsed it to 0                                                                          |
| Stored times resolve to the right frame                                 | Partly    | Frame-start times with at-or-before(t + min(1 ms, a quarter frame)): 0 wrong for frame-start writers and demuxed midpoints, except 1 of 480 paused frame-callback times, 4 to 7% for times inside a frame. A video element's current time fails every rule: 33 to 62% wrong after a pause         |
| Keyframes as ordinary annotations                                       | Confirmed | 1,000 tracks × 10 keyframes round-trip exactly through osdlabel's serializer: 14.8 MB of JSON against 4.1 MB for a separate track document. Today's deserializer strips the keyframe fields, so it needs a schema option                                                                          |
| DOM decorations are not the bottleneck                                  | Refuted   | 100 decorations cost 6 to 7 ms per frame including style and layout, about 40% of a 60 fps budget                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

**Independent review and fix:** three blocking findings. The nearest-frame lookup depended on how a time had been written; the frame-callback failures came from a harness timer; and the render-path recommendation, a separate video layer, rested on a 30 fps source that capped the metric. The fix pass defined stored times as frame starts with the tolerant lookup, re-measured the element with the timer fixed (6,240 of 6,240 callbacks), re-ran the render paths with a 60 fps source and a direct-draw host, which withdrew the separate layer, and built keyframes as ordinary annotations. Still unknown: real GPUs, Firefox and Safari, real footage and long files.

## Volumes and MPR: planes as ordinary images, Niivue for the 3D cell

Volumes compose with the 2D pipeline: each plane is an ordinary image in a stock cell, cross-view annotation is plain decoration providers, and Niivue serves the 3D cell. On the CPU, axial and coronal planes are interactive; sagittal and oblique are borderline, and the linked crosshair misses. Branch [claude/poc-volume](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/tree/claude/poc-volume) · [REPORT.md](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/blob/claude/poc-volume/apps/poc-volume/REPORT.md).

**Built:** `@osdlabel/spatial` (plane and volume geometry, no dependencies), `@osdlabel/volume` (slice-wise int16 volumes, CPU reslicing, permuted copies, dense and bricked label maps), `@osdlabel/mpr` (reversible plane IDs and cross-view providers), plus a WebGL2 reslicer, a canvas-backed tile source and a Niivue cell in the harness. Existing code: 6 source files, +43/−5.

| Hypothesis                                                                               | Verdict   | Evidence (512×512×400 int16 volume, medians of three runs, no other benchmark running)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| VO1: the CPU reslices orthogonal planes in under 10 ms, and oblique planes interactively | Partly    | While scrolling: axial 2.2 ms, coronal 4.6, sagittal 15.4 (6.2 from a sagittal-major copy, for 200 MiB more). Oblique trilinear 14 ms, 9 across 4 workers. Four canvas-drawer cells: axial 51 fps, coronal 44, sagittal 31, oblique 31, linked crosshair 21                                                                   |
| VO2: cross-view annotation renders through today's decoration layer                      | Partly    | 13 of 13 cross-view checks pass with the real tools on both drawers. It needed an optional imageId on the decoration context (8 lines). At 518 annotations, re-running every provider cost 18.6 ms per frame, against 3.2 ms memoised                                                                                         |
| VO3: Niivue embeds as the 3D cell and shares the volume buffer                           | Partly    | No CPU copy and one WebGL context. On the GPU it holds 3.0× the volume, 4.0× beside the WebGL reslicer and 8.0× with its drawing layer (1.68 GB). Overlay within 1 px; rotation speed cannot be judged on the software GPU                                                                                                    |
| Per-slice masks are slower than a label volume                                           | Refuted   | Re-measured fairly, masks, a dense volume and 32³ bricks painted within noise (0.02 to 0.1 ms per stamp). Masks and bricks displayed at parity with a lazily committed dense map; a committed dense map was about 3× slower sagittally (26.9 against 8.5 ms). | Plane IDs reload without a registry                                                      | Confirmed | Grid slices encode as vol:\<series>:axial:200, other planes as a quantized frame. All four MPR cells' IDs, the oblique one included, decode and re-encode identically                                                                                                                                                         |
| The canvas drawer keeps grids within the context budget                                  | Confirmed | With OpenSeadragon's default drawer, 20 stock cells left 4 blank; with the canvas drawer, 20 cells used 0 contexts and ran at 38 fps                                                                                                                                                                                          |

**Independent review and fix:** three blocking findings. The label-map speed ratios came from first-touch page faults and two different stamping algorithms; the frame rates skipped sagittal scrolling, the linked crosshair and annotation load; and "one GPU copy" ignored Niivue's own textures. The fix pass re-measured all three with three runs each, withdrew the ratios and the one-copy claim, added sagittal-major copies and sparse bricks, made plane IDs reversible, and split the sketched volume-osd package by concern. Still unknown: real GPUs, a storage layout that serves every view, and React re-running cross-view providers.

## SAM: assisted segmentation in a worker, on crops of the tile pyramid

SAM fits as a provider contract plus a tool in leaf packages. MobileSAM runs in a worker on crops read from the tile pyramid at model resolution. Encoding takes seconds and decoding is not interactive on the CPU, but cached clicks are quick and refinement after a zoom works. Branch [claude/poc-sam](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/tree/claude/poc-sam) · [REPORT.md](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/blob/claude/poc-sam/apps/poc-sam/REPORT.md).

**Built:** seven leaf packages: `@osdlabel/segmentation` (the contract, encode plan and embedding cache), `@osdlabel/region-reader`, `@osdlabel/mask-contour`, `@osdlabel/segmentation-onnx` (model adapters and a WebGPU gate), `@osdlabel/segmentation-worker`, `@osdlabel/segmentation-remote` (client adapters to existing services) and `@osdlabel/segmentation-fabric` (the tool). Existing code: 14 files, +38/−5.

| Hypothesis                                                                              | Verdict   | Evidence (synthetic 100,000×80,000 slide, 4-core machine under load)                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SA1: prompt decoding is interactive on the CPU, and light encoders take seconds at most | Partly    | Decoding took 130 to 400 ms on CPU WebAssembly, never under 50 ms. MobileSAM encoded in 2 to 9 s depending on threads and load, SAM 2 tiny in 7 to 22 s, ViT-B in 16 to 69 s                                                                                                           |
| SA2: gigapixel images need viewport-crop encoding with an embedding cache               | Confirmed | Cached clicks painted in 0.16 to 0.6 s. After a 4× zoom, one re-encode covered the view and the prompt, and the refined mask matched the first at IoU 0.992 (0.984 against ground truth)                                                                                               |
| SA3: SAM output maps onto #149's masks                                                  | Partly    | Masks commit as canonical RLE of 8 to 15 KB. At overview zoom two of four masks exceeded #149's cap (S1 at 2.3 gigapixels, 34×), and the prototype's fallback kept one ring, dropping 14 rings and 4 holes in S1's local runs. **Independent review and fix:** three blocking findings. The int8 rejection rested on a confounded comparison, the responsiveness result held only once tiles had loaded, and refinement after a pan or zoom was broken. Still unknown: real GPUs, medical images, and live MONAI Label or nnInteractive servers.

## Mask format: #149's cap, a resolution spike and the alternatives

\#149's masks cannot hold a region larger than about 2 mm square on a slide, whether the brush or SAM made it. A power-of-two downsample on each mask fixes that in about 450 lines of source. Branch [claude/spike-mask-resolution](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/tree/claude/spike-mask-resolution) · [SPIKE-REPORT.md](https://github.com/osdlabel/osdlabel/blob/claude/spike-mask-resolution/SPIKE-REPORT.md).

**Built:** a `downsample` field (an integer power of two, 1 meaning today's full resolution) on the mask geometry, payload and snapshot, with strokes rasterised on cells, the canonical and COCO codecs, the schema, the Fabric renderer, and a brush that chose the downsample from the view. One commit on #149 (58a379f): 30 files, +2,044/−76, 451 of them source lines, bindings untouched; every unit suite in both bindings and the Solid brush E2E pass. Also: probes that drive #149's own compiled mask package, a prototype of sparse full-resolution storage, and a vector-brush probe with Clipper2.

| Hypothesis                                                                          | Verdict   | Evidence (synthetic 100,000×80,000 slide unless stated)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| MK1: #149's cap limits only SAM output                                              | Refuted   | Driving #149's mask package the way the brush does, the largest square that commits is 8,142 px a side at radius 512, and a diagonal stroke is abandoned after 7,134 px on each axis. Decoding refuses a 47,677×47,678 box whatever it contains                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| MK2: re-encoding a finer crop at commit lifts the cap                               | Refuted   | The stored box is the object's full-resolution box. S1 filled its whole encoded crop, so a tight re-encode gives the same 234 px cells and the same 2.27-gigapixel box                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| MK3: a power-of-two downsample stores SAM's overview masks faithfully               | Confirmed | An S1-like mask (56,000×40,600 box, 33.9× the cap, 15 rings, 4 holes) at downsample 8: a 7,000×5,075 grid, 35.5 K characters, encoded in 165 to 178 ms and decoded in 14 to 18 ms, IoU 0.999, area error −1.05×10⁻⁴, every ring and hole kept, and 0 px placement error over 28,028 cell centres. At downsample 128: 1.6 K characters, IoU 0.986. The prototype's polygon fallback kept 1 of 15 rings and 0 of 4 holes, IoU 0.931 |
| MK4: older readers fail closed on coarser masks                                     | Confirmed | 33 coarser payloads (downsample 2 to 256, odd image sizes) through #149's decoder: none accepted silently, and deserialize skips each with a reason. Payloads at downsample 1 are byte-identical to #149's                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| MK5: choosing the downsample from the size of the view keeps brush strokes faithful | Refuted   | The same 324 px stroke committed at downsample 1, 2 or 4 depending on the cell's CSS size, its area varying by 3 to 9%. A mask begun zoomed out stayed coarse when refined at 40× (mean boundary error 4.0 px against 0.17). The recommended policy starts at full resolution and coarsens on overflow                                                                                                                            |
| MK6: sparse storage at full resolution is viable                                    | Partly    | Real S1 logits: 12.0 MB bit-packed or 95.7 MB as bytes in 256 px tiles (1,459 of 35,076 mixed), built in 1.05 s, and loaded from #149's payload in 0.24 s. | MK7: full resolution adds precision to SAM output                                   | Refuted   | S1's model cells are 234 px. Downsample 128 matches the full-resolution rendering at IoU 0.994, closer than nearest and bilinear upsampling of the same logits are to each other (0.987)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| MK8: polygons with holes can replace masks for large regions                        | Partly    | The S1-like mask traced at downsample 128: 21 rings, 4 holes, 1,378 vertices in 14.8 ms. A Clipper2 vector brush committed strokes on regions 1.5× to 37× the cap in 4.5 to 12.6 ms (median; at most 42 ms). **Independent review:** three critics, on correctness, on necessity and timing, and on user experience and interchange. The field held: their probes reproduced the old-reader behaviour, the cell counts, the chooser's numbers and the unit suites. They found the spike's brush rule wrong (MK5); that a required field alone does not stop code reading cells as image pixels, so the cell box should be its own nested type; that COCO import dropped the downsample and refused large masks, and COCO export needs a bound on its run count; and that SAM should take its downsample from the model's cell size rather than from the cap, since downsample 8 needs a 135 MB canvas, above iOS Safari's limit. They also corrected the roadmap: merging #149 releases the format, so marking it experimental is not a separate option, and the earlier 1.5 weeks predated the spike. Not done: parity tests in both bindings, E2E above downsample 1 (the dev images are small), browser render timings, re-gridding, and a whole-slide SEG export.

### Follow-up: sparse storage now, or later?

Once #149 was on hold, the question became whether to build sparse full-resolution storage before it merges, or ship the downsample field and add sparse storage later. **Review:** a decision memo, then one advocate for sparse now and one for doing less. Both accepted that sparse should not come first, and both rejected coarsening by default because it is the one irreversible step. They disagreed on how much forward compatibility to put in #149: the raster contract, moving the cap out of the schema, and keeping over-cap masks when reading. The roadmap keeps the contract and defers the other two. Corrections: sparse storage's first estimate was low (its own line items add up to 17.5 to 18 weeks, and a line-by-line check gives 17 to 24); the full downsample plan with every follow-on is 4.5 to 7.5 weeks, not 2 to 2.5; and an older reader rejects a whole document over one over-cap mask only when code calls the schema directly, since osdlabel's own reader skips that mask. Not measured: real GPUs, iOS, and the coarsening rule's own precision during painting, as opposed to one-shot quantisation of finished outlines.
